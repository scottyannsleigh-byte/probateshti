### Memorandum of law

9. **Standard.** Under Rule 1.500(d) the Court may set aside a default, and Florida public policy strongly favors adjudication on the merits: "if there be any reasonable doubt in the matter [of vacating a default], it should be resolved in favor of granting the application and allowing a trial upon the merits." *North Shore Hospital, Inc. v. Barber*, 143 So. 2d 849, 853 (Fla. 1962). A pre-judgment clerk's default is viewed with even greater liberality than a post-judgment motion. The movant shows (1) excusable neglect, (2) a meritorious defense, and (3) due diligence.
10. **Excusable neglect.** Defendant did not ignore this lawsuit — she answered it **within seven days of service**, and the record proves it: the Portal receipt (Ex. A), same-day e-service on Plaintiff's firm (Ex. C), and the Clerk's own correction notices (Ex. D, F, G). A filing misdirected between two *related, cross-referenced* cases by an unrepresented party — and thereafter processed under the Clerk's correction procedure — is precisely the "clerical or secretarial error, reasonable misunderstanding, [or] system gone awry" that Florida courts hold excusable. *E.g., Somero v. Hendry Gen. Hosp.*, 467 So. 2d 1103, 1106 (Fla. 4th DCA 1985); *Elliott v. Aurora Loan Servs., LLC*, 31 So. 3d 304 (Fla. 4th DCA 2010).
11. **The Answer relates back to June 11, 2026 — before both the deadline and the default.** Under Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.525(f)(2)(A) (as amended effective July 1, 2025), a filer whose document is placed in the correction queue may "file a new document that remedies the reasons stated in the notice but is otherwise substantially identical, which if successfully filed, **relates back to the date of the unsuccessful filing attempt**." Defendant's tendered Answer remedies the noticed defect (wrong case) exactly as the Clerk's June 15 notice instructed, and is otherwise substantially identical; it therefore relates back to June 11, 2026 — a **timely** date. An answer deemed filed before entry of default defeats the default entirely. Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.500(c) ("A party may plead or otherwise defend at any time before default is entered.").
12. **Plaintiff had actual, contemporaneous knowledge Defendant was defending.** Plaintiff's counsel's firm received the Answer by Portal e-service on June 11 (Ex. C) and corresponded with Defendant about her defense (Ex. H). There is no surprise and no prejudice to Plaintiff in deciding this case on its merits; the default would work an extreme forfeiture — the loss of Defendant's home — over a docketing error the Clerk had already given Defendant leave to correct.
13. **Meritorious defense.** The tendered Answer (Ex. I) pleads, with the chain-of-title statement and instruments attached as required by § 66.021(7), Florida Statutes, that: (a) on September 13, 2025, the decedent executed an enhanced life estate ("Lady Bird") deed conveying the remainder in the subject property to Defendant effective at his death, witnessed by two subscribing witnesses, one of whom has executed a sworn Proof of Execution already accepted in the related probate file; (b) upon the decedent's death the remainder vested in Defendant by operation of law, outside probate; (c) independently, the property was the decedent's **protected homestead**, which "is not an asset of the estate," and the personal representative has no right to its possession, §§ 733.607(1), 733.608(1)–(2), Fla. Stat.; *Clifton v. Clifton*, 553 So. 2d 192 (Fla. 5th DCA 1989); and (d) in ejectment the plaintiff must recover on the strength of his own title, *Fla. Finance Co. v. Sheffield*, 48 So. 42 (Fla. 1908) — a chain that here terminates with the decedent. Notably, the decedent's own title arrived by the identical instrument: the recorded 2020 enhanced life estate deed from William F. Melillo to the decedent (OR Book 21077, Page 809, Instr. #2020208528).
14. **Due diligence.** Defendant objected to the default in writing within hours, resubmitted within the Clerk's window, and files this motion within days of the default's entry.
15. Defendant further notes the default was entered against "**Brandi Rogers**," a misspelling of Defendant's legal name that Defendant had already corrected to Plaintiff's counsel in writing.
