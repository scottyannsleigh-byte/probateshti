Amerijet*, 631 So. 2d 371 (Fla. 4th DCA 1994)), a defendant who has served **any** document — even an informal letter to counsel — cannot be defaulted by the clerk and is entitled to notice and court involvement. ## Part 2 — Why your deed matters so much (the merits)

If the September 13, 2025 deed was **actually signed by Rick in the presence of two witnesses and delivered**, then:

1. **It's a valid Florida "Lady Bird" (enhanced life estate) deed.** These are recognized by long-standing Florida law (*Oglesby v. Lee*, 73 So. 840 (Fla. 1917); Florida Uniform Title Standards 6.10–6.11, which expressly cover homestead). Rick kept the right to sell or undo it during his life; he didn't; so at his death the remainder **vested in you automatically, outside probate**.
2. **Notarization is NOT required for validity.** Fla. Stat. § 689.01 requires only a writing signed by the grantor before **two subscribing witnesses**. A notary (§ 695.03) is needed only to make the deed *recordable*.
3. **Non-recording does not help the estate.** Fla. Stat. § 695.01 voids an unrecorded deed only against *creditors and subsequent purchasers for value without notice*. **Heirs, devisees, and the personal representative are none of those** — they stand in Rick's shoes and take subject to his delivered deed.
4. **Independently of the deed — homestead.** If the house was Rick's protected homestead (he lived there; it had a homestead exemption through 2025), it is **not an asset of the estate at all** (*Clifton v. Clifton*, 553 So. 2d 192 (Fla. 5th DCA 1989); *McKean v. Warburton*, 919 So. 2d 341 (Fla. 2005)). The PR's statutory possession power **expressly excludes protected homestead** (Fla. Stat. §§ 733.607(1), 733.608(1)), and § 733.608(2) lets a PR take custody of apparent homestead **only if it is "not occupied by a person who appears to have an interest in the property."** You occupy it and claim an interest. In ejectment, the plaintiff must win **on the strength of his own title** (*Florida Finance Co. v. Sheffield*, 48 So. 42 (Fla. 1908)) — and the PR's chain of title arguably dead-ends at Rick's death.

**Both defenses go in your affirmative defenses**: (1) title passed to Defendant at death under the enhanced life estate deed; (2) the property was decedent's protected homestead, is not an estate asset, and the PR lacks a superior right of possession.

**Anticipate their attacks** (be ready, don't be surprised): forgery/handwriting challenge, lack of capacity, undue influence, non-delivery, witness credibility (one witness is your son). **Safeguard the ORIGINAL signed deed** — make copies, photograph it, and consider leaving the original in a safe deposit box or with counsel. If a signed original does not exist, tell your legal aid attorney immediately — the strategy changes.

## Part 3 — The urgent risk nobody has addressed: a sale to a third party

Your deed is unrecorded. Under § 695.01, if the PR sells the house to a **bona fide purchaser for value without notice**, that purchaser could cut off your unrecorded interest. The PR has already sold two other properties this spring. Two protections (discuss with counsel THIS WEEK):

1. **Record the deed.** A deed without notarization can be recorded by having its execution **proved by a subscribing witness** under Fla. Stat. § 695.03 — Christopher Kraft's sworn proof of execution is exactly that mechanism. Recording puts the world on notice. (Minimal cost: recording fees + minimum documentary stamps on a $10-consideration deed.) Only record the genuine, signed original instrument.
2. **Record a Notice of Lis Pendens** (Fla. Stat. § 48.23) once your quiet-title/declaratory counterclaim is filed in 26-002613-CI. A lis pendens warns any buyer that title is in litigation. Because your claim is founded on a "duly recorded instrument" only after the deed is recorded, sequence matters — this is precisely the kind of step a legal aid lawyer can do correctly in an afternoon.

Either step dramatically reduces the risk of waking up to a closed sale.

## Part 4 — The counterclaims and the $395

- File the fee-free Answer + Affirmative Defenses **now**; file the **counterclaim** (quiet title / declaratory judgment on the deed) as a separate amended pleading with either (a) the $395 fee, or (b) an **Application for Determination of Civil Indigent Status** (Fla. Stat. § 57.082).
- Indigency standard: income ≤ **200% of the federal poverty guidelines** for your household. The homestead and one vehicle (≤ $5,000 equity) are **excluded** from the asset test. The clerk's determination is ministerial and fast; the form is free (mypinellasclerk.org → Forms → "Determination of Indigency"; also available at filing time in the portal — on the Fees and Payments page choose "Request Waiver"/indigency option).
- Note: because the counterclaim concerns real property, the clerk could theoretically apply graduated fees ($395/$900/$1,900 by value) under § 28.241(1)(c)2. The clerk has quoted $395 so far. Indigent status moots the issue.
- The probate-queue item (Filing #250196245): once your answer is safely filed in 26-002613-CI, the queued probate copy can be **abandoned** (take no action) — or, if the clerk refuses relation-back or bounces you again, file a **Motion for Review of the clerk's action under Rule 2.525(f)(2)(B)**, which freezes the queued document until a judge rules. Worth knowing: the July 1 fee-based bounce is questionable — the amended Rule 2.525(f)(1) lists seven exclusive grounds for the correction queue, and a missing fee is not one of them.

## Part 5 — The probate case: what to file there (and what not to)

- **Do NOT litigate title in probate.** You claim *adversely* to the estate ("stranger to the estate") — that fight belongs in the circuit civil case the PR himself chose. Duplicating it in probate invites delay.
- **DO consider a Petition to Determine Protected Homestead Status of Real Property (Fla. Prob. R. 5.405)** — an "interested person" may file it, and a determination that the house was protected homestead destroys the PR's possession claim regardless of the deed fight. Filing fee for a petition by an interested person is ~$395 in Pinellas (same indigency waiver applies). Get legal aid's view on timing/standing first.
- **A caveat (§ 731.110) is moot** — it only operates *before* a will is admitted/PR appointed, which happened in January.
- **Stay visible in probate**: you already have the Kraft proof on file. Make sure you're on the e-service list for everything (you appear to be). Watch for any petition touching the real property — you would have grounds to object and demand adjudication of homestead status first.
- The creditor claims (two hospitals, Medicaid/AHCA) generally **cannot reach protected homestead** (Art. X, § 4, Fla. Const.) — another reason the homestead label matters.

## Part 7 — If a default has ALREADY been entered

Don't panic — move fast. File a **Motion to Vacate Default under Rule 1.500(d)** (and 1.540(b) if any judgment followed). Florida strongly prefers merits decisions; you need:
1. **Excusable neglect** — the wrong-case e-filing + correction-queue chain (documented by the portal emails) is a classic "system gone awry" scenario; you acted within the clerk's own correction process;
2. **Meritorious defense** — the Lady Bird deed + homestead defenses (attach the deed, Kraft proof, and a short affidavit);
3. **Due diligence** — you responded within days of learning of the default motion (June 29–30 emails; immediate filing).
Also argue the default was **void or irregular** because you had served documents, so only a court could default you, with notice (Rule 1.500(b), *Picchi*).
