1. EJECTMENT ELEMENTS AND THE PR'S STANDING PROBLEM

Ejectment is governed by Fla. Stat. ch. 66 (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099%2F0066%2F0066.html). Under 66.021(2), "a person with a superior right to possession of real property may maintain an action of ejectment." The plaintiff must prove (a) legal title/superior right of possession, (b) present right to immediate possession, and (c) wrongful withholding by the defendant. The century-old rule is that an ejectment plaintiff "must recover upon the strength of his own title, and not upon the weakness of his adversary's." Florida Finance Co. v. Sheffield, 56 Fla. 285, 48 So. 42 (1908) (https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/florida-finance-co-v-892168331). Procedurally critical: 66.021(4) requires BOTH the complaint and the answer to contain a chronological chain-of-title statement (grantors, grantees, recording dates, book/page or instrument numbers) with copies of each instrument attached; unrecorded instruments on which a party relies must also be attached. 66.021(5) lets either party test the legal sufficiency of the opponent's title instruments by pretrial motion. So Rodgers should (i) attach the Sept. 13, 2025 quitclaim/Lady Bird deed to her answer as her chain of title, and (ii) move to test Hewett's chain — which necessarily dead-ends at the decedent, because a PR takes no title to homestead.

The PR-standing point is the heart of the defense. Fla. Stat. 733.607(1) (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799%2F0733%2FSections%2F0733.607.html) gives the personal representative the right to possession of the decedent's property "except the protected homestead." Fla. Stat. 733.608(1) repeats the exception ("all real and personal property of the decedent, except the protected homestead"), and 733.608(2) permits the PR to take custody of property that "reasonably appears to be protected homestead" ONLY IF it "is not occupied by a person who appears to have an interest in the property," and then only for the limited purpose of "preserving, insuring, and protecting it" — not to administer, rent, or sell it (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799%2F0733%2FSections%2F0733.608.html). Here the property IS occupied by a person claiming an interest (Rodgers, as Lady Bird remainderman), so even the narrow preservation power does not authorize dispossessing her.

Case law: protected homestead "is not an asset of the estate" and passes outside probate. Clifton v. Clifton, 553 So. 2d 192, 194 n.3 (Fla. 5th DCA 1989) ("Homestead property, whether devised or not, passes outside of the probate estate."); Cavanaugh v. Cavanaugh, 542 So. 2d 1345, 1351 (Fla. 1st DCA 1989) (homestead "in no wise an asset of the estate"); McKean v. Warburton, 919 So. 2d 341, 344 (Fla. 2005) (homestead is not part of the probate estate and passes outside it unless validly devised to one other than an heir when freely devisable); Harrell v. Snyder, 913 So. 2d 749 (Fla. 5th DCA 2005) (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1381769.html) (homestead does not become part of the probate estate "unless a testamentary disposition is permitted and is made to someone other than an heir"; PR's possession power over apparent homestead is limited to preservation pending homestead determination); Estate of Shefner v. Shefner-Holden, 2 So. 3d 1076, 1078 (Fla. 3d DCA 2009). Practical upshot: if the Seminole property was Melillo's protected homestead at death, title passed AT THE MOMENT OF DEATH either to Rodgers (if the Lady Bird deed is valid) or to his heirs (if it is not) — in neither scenario to the estate — and Hewett as PR cannot plead or prove the legal title/right-of-possession element of ejectment. The anticipated reply is that homestead status and the deed's validity are unadjudicated; but that only underscores that the title dispute must be resolved (quiet title/declaratory relief, or a probate homestead-status determination under Fla. Prob. R. 5.405) before anyone can eject her, and the burden in ejectment stays on the plaintiff.

2. LADY BIRD / ENHANCED LIFE ESTATE DEED

Florida has recognized enhanced life estate deeds since Oglesby v. Lee, 73 Fla. 39, 73 So. 840 (1917) (grantor may reserve a life estate plus the power to sell/divest the remainder without the remainderman's joinder). There is no governing statute; validity rests on case law and Florida Uniform Title Standards 6.10-6.12 (RPPTL Section) which expressly approve enhanced life estate deeds for both non-homestead (6.10) and HOMESTEAD (6.11) property (see https://www.flprobatelitigation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/837/2023/07/UTC-lady-bird-deed-STANDARD-6.10.pdf; https://www.alperlaw.com/estate-planning/florida-lady-bird-deed/). If the grantor dies without having exercised the reserved power, the remainder vests in the remainderman automatically at death by operation of law; no probate, PR deed, or court order is needed. A quitclaim form is fine — Florida deed validity does not depend on warranty language.

Execution formalities: Fla. Stat. 689.01 (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699%2F0689%2FSections%2F0689.01.html) requires a freehold conveyance to be in writing, signed by the grantor "in the presence of two subscribing witnesses." Notarization is NOT among the 689.01 validity requirements. Acknowledgment before a notary (Fla. Stat. 695.03) is required only to ENTITLE THE DEED TO RECORDING and thereby impart constructive notice; a deed that is signed, twice-witnessed, and DELIVERED is fully effective to pass legal title between the parties even if never acknowledged and never recorded. See Fla. AGO (unacknowledged instrument "may be valid and effective between the parties" but is not entitled to record) (https://www.myfloridalegal.com/ag-opinions/recordation-and-unacknowledged-option-to-purchase); Chase Federal Sav. & Loan v. Schreiber, 479 So. 2d 90 (Fla. 1985) (recording/acknowledgment doctrine); Bould v. Coe, 63 So. 2d 273 (Fla. 1953) (validity turns on DELIVERY during the grantor's life, not recording).

Non-recording: Fla. Stat. 695.01(1) makes an unrecorded conveyance void only "against creditors or subsequent purchasers for a valuable consideration and without notice" (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699%2F0695%2F0695.html). Heirs, devisees, and the personal representative are none of these — they are volunteers/successors who stand in the decedent-grantor's shoes and take subject to his prior delivered deed, recorded or not. (Estate creditors are also largely irrelevant here because protected homestead is exempt from creditor claims under Art. X, § 4(a)-(b), and the exemption inures to heirs, Snyder v. Davis, 699 So. 2d 999 (Fla. 1997).) THE REAL BATTLEGROUNDS will be factual: (a) DELIVERY — the estate will argue a "pocket deed" was never delivered (Bould v. Coe; Fla. Bar Journal, "Failure to Deliver: The Problem with Pocket Deeds," https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/failure-to-deliver-the-problem-with-pocket-deeds-and-a-review-of-alternatives/). Rodgers's possession of the original deed raises a rebuttable presumption of delivery, and the grantor's reservation of an enhanced life estate is fully consistent with intent to deliver a presently operative instrument; (b) whether BOTH witnesses (Scott J. Rodgers and Christopher T. Kraft) actually signed in the grantor's presence at execution; and (c) authenticity/capacity/undue influence. She should preserve the original deed, get sworn statements from both witnesses, and expect to prove execution at trial through their testimony (Fla. Stat. 92.05 also permits proof of instruments through subscribing witnesses; and note the 5-year curative statute, Fla. Stat. 95.231, cures witness/acknowledgment defects only after a deed has been RECORDED for 5 years — not yet applicable).

3. RECORDING AN UNNOTARIZED DEED — PROOF BY SUBSCRIBING WITNESS

The correct mechanism is Fla. Stat. 695.03 itself: to be recordable, an instrument's execution must be "acknowledged by the party executing it, PROVED BY A SUBSCRIBING WITNESS to it, or legalized or authenticated" before an authorized officer (in-state: judge, clerk or deputy clerk, notary, or civil-law notary, under seal — 695.03(1); see https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699%2F0695%2FSections%2F0695.03.html). Fla. Stat. 695.09 requires the officer taking the proof to know or have satisfactory proof "that the person offering to make proof is one of the subscribing witnesses to such instrument." Mechanically: Kraft (or Scott Rodgers) appears before a notary; swears that he saw Richard Melillo sign the deed and that he and the other witness subscribed as witnesses in the grantor's presence; the notary completes a certificate of proof under seal; the certificate is attached to the original deed; and the deed plus certificate is recorded in the Pinellas County Official Records (recorder formatting requirements, including legibly printed names AND post-office addresses of the two witnesses, per Fla. Stat. 695.26 — witness addresses became mandatory for deeds recorded after Jan. 1, 2024). Two cautions: (i) the "Proof of Execution" e-filed into probate case 26-000874-ES is a COURT FILING, not a recording — it does not put the deed in the Official Records or impart constructive notice; the deed must be recorded with the Clerk's recording department; (ii) recording now still cannot prejudice any bona fide purchaser/creditor who intervened before recording, but none appears to exist — the adverse parties (estate/heirs) are not protected classes under 695.01. Also note 695.03(4)'s validation clause: recorded instruments may not be denied constructive-notice effect for alleged technical noncompliance with 695.03.

4. HOMESTEAD DEVISE/ALIENATION RESTRICTIONS — UNMARRIED DECEDENT

Art. X, § 4(c), Fla. Const. (https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/florida-constitution1968-revision/fl-const-art-10-sect-4/) and Fla. Stat. 732.4015 (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799%2F0732%2FSections%2F0732.4015.html) restrict DEVISE only "if the owner is survived by spouse or minor child." An unmarried owner with no MINOR children may freely devise or convey homestead to anyone; ADULT children have no protected interest and impose no restriction. City National Bank of Florida v. Tescher, 578 So. 2d 701 (Fla. 1991). Two independent reasons the restrictions don't threaten the Lady Bird deed here: (1) Melillo was unmarried, so unless he left a minor child the restriction never attaches; (2) even for owners WITH a spouse/minor child, an inter vivos CONVEYANCE is not a "devise" — Art. X, § 4(c) expressly lets the owner "alienate the homestead by mortgage, sale or gift" (joined by a spouse only if married), and Title Standard 6.11 approves Lady Bird deeds of homestead by unmarried owners. If (and only if) the deed fails, intestate homestead descends at death directly to his heirs — adult children, per 732.103 — outside probate, with the creditor exemption inuring to them (Snyder v. Davis, 699 So. 2d 999 (Fla. 1997); Fla. Stat. 732.401 governs only the spouse/lineal-descendant life-estate split, inapplicable to an unmarried decedent). Even in that fallback, the PR still has no possessory right; only the heirs themselves could sue.

(a) Affirmative defenses in the CI case: lack of standing/failure to prove legal title (PR of homestead); title vested in defendant via the delivered Lady Bird deed (this is a claim of LEGAL title, not merely equitable — an unrecorded delivered deed passes legal title); failure of the statutory chain-of-title pleading (66.021(4)); and equitable defenses (estoppel, unclean hands), which are expressly pleadable in any civil action since the merger of law and equity, Fla. The $395 counterclaim fee the Pinellas clerk demanded corresponds to filing a claim for affirmative relief (counterclaim/cross-claim fee under Fla.
