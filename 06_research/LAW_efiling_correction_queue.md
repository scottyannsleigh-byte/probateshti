GOVERNING LAW CHANGED JULY 1, 2025 — THE NEW RULE, NOT THE OLD "5-DAY" STANDARDS, CONTROLS THESE JUNE 2026 EVENTS

In In re: Amendments to Florida Rules of General Practice and Judicial Administration, No. SC2023-1401 (Fla., corrected opinion), the Florida Supreme Court rewrote Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.525, effective July 1, 2025 at 12:01 a.m. (opinion: https://supremecourt.flcourts.gov/content/download/2452359/opinion/Opinion_SC2023-1401.pdf; current rule text: https://floridacourtrules.com/general-practice-and-judicial-administration/rule-2.525/). This is why the Pinellas clerk's notices gave "30 calendar days" rather than the old 5-business-day correction window. I extracted the appendix text of the amended rule verbatim from the opinion PDF.

1) RELATION BACK — YES, BY EXPRESS RULE TEXT. Amended Rule 2.525(f)(2) (verbatim from the SC2023-1401 appendix): "If subdivision (f)(1) applies and the relevant case can be identified, the clerk, portal, or other designated electronic court filing facility will immediately notify the filer and all persons initially served with that document specifying the reason the filing was unsuccessful and has been placed in the correction queue... The clerk, portal, or other designated electronic court filing facility will hold the document in the correction queue for an additional 30 days from the date the filer was notified of the unsuccessful filing. During those 30 days, the person who submitted it may: (A) file a new document that remedies the reasons stated in the notice but is otherwise substantially identical, which if successfully filed, relates back to the date of the unsuccessful filing attempt and permits disposal of the document in the correction queue; (B) file a motion for review of the clerk's action, attaching a copy of the document, in which case the clerk, portal, or other designated electronic court filing facility must hold the document until the motion is decided by the court; or (C) take no action under subdivisions (f)(2)(A) or (f)(2)(B), thereby abandoning the document." Amended subdivision (e) ("Filed Date and Time") separately provides that "A successfully filed document's file date and time is the earlier of the date and time... stamp applied to the document by the portal or other designated electronic court filing facility." The AOSC20-77 Florida Courts E-Filing Portal Standards (v19.0, Aug. 2020, https://supremecourt.flcourts.gov/content/download/642993/file/AOSC20-77-Corrected.pdf) confirm at §1.2.4 (Electronic Notification of Receipt): the acknowledgment "must include the date and time the submission was received which is the official filing date/time," and §1.2.5 (Review by Clerk of Court) describes the correction queue (note: the section is 1.2.5 in AOSC20-77 and 3.1.8 in the Nov. 2016 v17.0 Standards — not 2.2.5; the older standards' 5-business-day period is superseded by the rule's 30 days). Applied here: Brandi's answer was submitted to the portal June 11, 2026, 7:55 p.m.; if a substantially identical answer is successfully filed within the current 30-day window, it is deemed filed June 11, 2026 as a matter of rule text — before the June 29 default motion.

2) REDIRECTING TO THE CORRECT CASE — PERMITTED, AND THE RULE ITSELF CONTEMPLATES A "NEW DOCUMENT." The relation-back mechanism in (f)(2)(A) is not tied to the portal's correction-queue interface: the cure is to "file a new document that remedies the reasons stated in the notice but is otherwise substantially identical." Where the noticed defect is "wrong case," the remedy is filing the same answer into the right case (26-002613-CI); that filing relates back by operation of the rule. The Portal Filer User Manual (Aug. 2023, pp. 150-160, https://documents.myflcourtaccess.com/uploads/2023/12/Portal-E-Filer-User-Manual-August-2023_wip.pdf) confirms the correction queue reopens the entire filing path (Case Information, parties, documents, e-service, Review & Submit with "Revise" buttons) and expressly lists "wrong case number was selected" as a standard correction-queue reason the filer fixes on resubmission. The manual also states the timestamp rules: "If you replace the document, the official file stamp date and time will remain the same as when originally submitted"; deleting/adding documents produces a new stamp. Practical guidance: the safest path is BOTH (a) immediately e-file the answer directly into 26-002613-CI as a new submission expressly invoking Rule 2.525(f)(2)(A) relation back to June 11, 2026 (attach the correction-queue notices), and (b) if the clerk resists, file a motion for review under (f)(2)(B), which freezes the queued document until a judge rules. Note the answer alone carries no filing fee — the $395 (Fla. Stat. §28.241) attaches only to the counterclaim/counterpetition. She can sever them: file the fee-free answer now to defeat default, and file the counterclaim with the fee or with a civil indigency application under Fla. Stat. §57.082 (pro se filers may apply through the portal), which eliminates the fee obstacle.

IMPORTANT DEFECT IN THE JULY 1 BOUNCE: a missing filing fee is NOT one of the seven exclusive correction-queue grounds in amended Rule 2.525(f)(1)(A)-(G) (wrong/unidentifiable case number; missing/wrong case style; multiple docs as one; one doc as multiple; proposed order; illegible/corrupt/blank; barred by court order or incapable of being filed in the CMS). Subdivision (f)(3) commands: "Except for the reasons set forth in subdivision (f)(1) of this rule, the clerk will docket all documents submitted for filing," with the clerk limited to annotating noncompliance for the court to address. The pre-2025 standards expressly allowed pending for "an incorrect filing fee" (AOSC20-77 §1.2.5); the 2025 amendment deleted that authority (see Collier Clerk analysis, "The End of the Safety Net," https://www.collierclerk.com/the-end-of-the-safety-net-what-floridas-new-e-filing-rules-mean-for-attorneys/). The clerk's July 1 fee-based rejection is therefore challengeable by (f)(2)(B) motion for review. This is consistent with Strax Rejuvenation & Aesthetics Inst., Inc. v. Shield, 49 So. 3d 741 (Fla. 2010) (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-supreme-court/1539745.html), where the Court held a document is filed when actually received by the clerk and the clerk's stamp is rebuttable, approving Weintraub v. Alter (3d DCA) (notice of appeal timely though the clerk refused it over the form of fee payment) — fee-processing problems do not defeat the fact of filing.

3) IF THE 30-DAY WINDOW LAPSES: under (f)(2)(C) inaction means "abandoning the document." The Portal Manual: once moved to the Abandoned Filing Queue, "no updates/corrections can be made to that submission on the Portal by the filer. It is deemed to have been abandoned and unable to be filed into the official court record." The relation-back RIGHT dies with abandonment. But the June 11 date remains evidenced by: the portal Notification of Electronic Filing/acknowledgment (the "official filing date/time" under AOSC20-77 §1.2.4), the e-service of the answer on Cavonis and the dhclaw staff on June 11 (the manual confirms correction-queue notices and NEFs go to the whole e-service list), and the ACCEPTED companion filing #250196910 (Kraft proof of execution, accepted June 15). Those records support excusable neglect/due diligence even if relation back is lost. A chaining question exists: her June 29 resubmission (within the first window) was itself queued July 1 with a fresh 30-day window; textually each cure "relates back to the date of the unsuccessful filing attempt," supporting a chain back to June 11 — no appellate decision yet construes the post-2025 rule (it is too new), so a court could limit relation back to June 29; either date precedes entry of any default.

4) DEFAULT PROTECTION. (a) Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.500(a) permits a CLERK default only when the defendant "has failed to file or serve any document in the action." Brandi SERVED her answer on plaintiff's counsel June 11 via portal e-service. Under Picchi v. Barnett Bank of South Florida, N.A., 521 So. 2d 1090 (Fla. 1988) (https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/1988/70464-0.html), a defendant who has served (even without filing) any paper cannot be defaulted by the clerk; the plaintiff must apply to the COURT under 1.500(b) and give notice, and a clerk default entered despite served papers must be vacated. Counsel here had actual service of the answer and received the correction-queue notices (Rule 2.525(f)(2) requires notice to "all persons initially served"), so a clerk default is improper and vulnerable regardless of the correction-queue outcome. (b) Rule 1.500(c): "A party may plead or otherwise defend at any time before default is entered" — filing the answer in 26-002613-CI before any default is entered defeats the pending motion outright. (c) If a default nonetheless enters, Florida's strong policy favoring adjudication on the merits applies: North Shore Hospital, Inc. v. Barber, 143 So. 2d 849 (Fla. 1962) (reasonable doubt resolved in favor of vacatur and trial on the merits); misdirected/misfiled papers are classic excusable neglect. (d) Strax (above) supports treating the portal submission date as the filing date notwithstanding clerk processing errors. No published Florida appellate decision yet applies the July 2025 correction-queue relation-back provision, but its text is unambiguous and self-executing.
