1. FILING FEE FOR A DEFENDANT'S ANSWER IN CIRCUIT CIVIL (PINELLAS)

The single most important finding: A DEFENDANT PAYS NOTHING TO FILE AN ANSWER WITH AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES. Fla. Stat. 28.241(3) states verbatim: "A filing fee may not be imposed upon a party for responding by pleading, motion, or other paper to a civil or criminal action, suit, proceeding, or appeal in a circuit court." (https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0028/Sections/0028.241.html). The premise in the task question is slightly off: 28.241(1)(b) is NOT a defendant appearance fee — it is the $295 fee for actions under chapters 39, 61, 741, 742, 747, 752, 753 (family-type cases). There is no defendant "appearance fee" in Florida circuit civil.

THE $395 IS THE COUNTERCLAIM/COUNTERPETITION FEE. Fla. Stat. 28.241(1)(c)1. (2025) requires a party who files a pleading "for affirmative relief by cross-claim, counterclaim, counterpetition, or third-party complaint" to pay $395. Sub-subparagraph (1)(c)2. imposes graduated fees in real property or mortgage foreclosure actions based on claim value: $395 (claim $50,000 or less), $900 ($50,000.01–$250,000), $1,900 (over $250,000) (https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/28.241). The Pinellas Clerk fee schedule (mypinellasclerk.org FeeSchedule.pdf, brochure rev. 01/21) matches: "CIRCUIT CIVIL COURT RECORDS — Circuit Civil - claim > $15,000, not more than 5 defendants $400.00; Circuit Civil - Counterclaim/Crossclaim/Counterpetition/Third-Party Complaint $395.00; Real Property or Foreclosure Action, value of claim $50,000 or less $400.00 [with counterclaim tiers $395/$900/$1,900]."

Practical consequence for Ms. Rodgers in 26-002613-CI: her "Answer, Defenses, Counterclaims, Jury Demand" was priced at $395 solely because it contains COUNTERCLAIMS. If she strips the counterclaims and files only "Answer and Affirmative Defenses" (her Lady Bird deed is usable defensively as an affirmative defense to ejectment — a defense does not require a counterclaim fee), the filing costs $0 and cannot be bounced for nonpayment. She can assert the deed affirmatively later (or in probate) once a fee waiver is granted. CAUTION on the graduated tier: because the ejectment/quiet-title dispute concerns real property, a counterpetition seeking title relief could theoretically be assessed under the (1)(c)2 graduated tiers ($900/$1,900 by value); however, both the Pinellas probate clerk's actual demand and the standard clerk practice quoted her exactly $395, and the base counterclaim line is $395.

JURY DEMAND: No fee. Fla. Stat. 28.241 contains no jury trial or jury demand fee, and the Pinellas circuit civil fee schedule lists none. (County court/small claims jury deposits under other statutes are inapplicable to a circuit CI case.) Including "Jury Demand" in the answer costs nothing.

2. CIVIL INDIGENCY — FLA. STAT. 57.082

Qualification (57.082(2)(a)1.): income "equal to or below 200 percent of the then-current federal poverty guidelines prescribed for the size of the household of the applicant by the United States Department of Health and Human Services." (For 2026, 200% of the FPG for a one-person household is roughly $31,000/yr — confirm the current HHS figure.) Presumption of NON-indigency (57.082(2)(a)2.) if the applicant "owns, or has equity in, any intangible or tangible personal property or real property... having a net equity value of $2,500 or more, excluding the value of the person's homestead and one vehicle having a net value not exceeding $5,000." Critically for Rodgers: homestead is excluded from the asset test, and her interest in the Seminole house is disputed/unrecorded anyway.

How to apply: file the Supreme Court "APPLICATION FOR DETERMINATION OF CIVIL INDIGENT STATUS" (official form at https://flcourts-media.flcourts.gov/content/download/403010/file/indigent_application.pdf; Pinellas posts its own copy at https://www.mypinellasclerk.org/Portals/0/Forms/DeterminationofIndigency.pdf). The form asks for dependents, net income, other income, assets, liabilities; is signed under penalty of first-degree misdemeanor for false statements. It can be filed in person at the clerk (Civil Court Records, Room 170, 315 Court St., Clearwater), by mail, or THROUGH THE E-FILING PORTAL AT SUBMISSION TIME (see part 4). The clerk "shall assist a person who... requests assistance in completing the application" (57.082(1)(b)).

Speed and nature of determination: "The determination of indigent status is a ministerial act of the clerk and not a decision based on further investigation or the exercise of independent judgment" (57.082(2)(d)) — the clerk compares the application's numbers to the poverty guidelines; in practice determination is made when the application is processed (same day to a few days). The form itself has the "CLERK'S DETERMINATION" block ("Indigent / Not Indigent"). If found NOT indigent, "APPLICANTS FOUND NOT TO BE INDIGENT MAY SEEK REVIEW BY A JUDGE BY ASKING FOR A HEARING TIME. THERE IS NO FEE FOR THIS REVIEW" (form; 57.082(2)(e)).

What approval gets her — waiver vs. deferral: the form's Notice states: "If you qualify for civil indigence, the filing and summons fees are waived; other costs and fees are not waived." So the $395 counterclaim fee and any summons fees are WAIVED; other costs (e.g., service by sheriff beyond summons issuance in some counties, copies, transcript costs) are handled through a payment plan under 57.082(5) — the applicant acknowledges on the form: "I understand that I will be required to make payments for costs to the clerk in accordance with §57.082(5), Florida Statutes." Pinellas charges a one-time $25 "Setting up a payment plan" processing charge (fee schedule).

**Corrected/qualified statement:** Accurate as to portal mechanics, but three qualifications matter here: (1) The FAQ's waiver form language is generic ("Affidavit of Indigency" / "Attach Waiver Form"); the Application for Determination of Civil Indigent Status (s. 57.082, Fla. Stat.) is the correct standard trial-court form but is not named in the FAQ. (2) The submission-time file stamp is provisional in practice: the clerk may still route the filing to the correction queue (per Rule 2.525(f)), and if the deficiency is not cured within 30 days the filing is abandoned and the stamp preserves nothing; the stamp survives only via acceptance or a timely corrected refiling that relates back. (3) As applied to Brandi Rodgers: her stamps to date are in the probate case (26-000874-ES), not the ejectment case (26-002613-CI); a fee-waiver request cannot retroactively place an answer on file in the CI case. To get any file stamp effective against the pending default motion, she must submit the answer into 26-002613-CI itself (with Request Fee Waiver + the s. 57.082 application on the Fees and Payments page), and any relation-back argument under Rule 2.525(f)(2) for the June 11 mis-filed submission is uncertain because the clerk's stated remedy was resubmission into a different case, not correction within the same case.

**Verifier's analysis:** CONFIRMED with qualifications. I downloaded the cited PDF (https://documents.myflcourtaccess.com/uploads/2024/08/FAQ_For_Filers_Nov_2021-tagged.pdf) and extracted its text. Both quoted elements exist verbatim: (1) p.7: "How may I have filing fees waived if filing on behalf of an entity exempt pursuant to s. 28.345, Florida Statutes or if Filing an Affidavit of Indigency? On the Fees and Payments page: Request Fee Waiver / Select Waiver Reason / Attach Waiver Form"; (2) p.8 ("How do you date stamp the document you filed?"): "The Portal will affix in the upper left-hand corner of the document the official file stamp date and time of when that submission reached the Portal. That is the official file stamp date and time of when the documents are filed and is in the Eastern Time Zone." The legal inference is also independently supported: Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.525(e) (verified at floridacourtrules.com/general-practice-and-judicial-administration/rule-2.525/) makes the portal's date-time stamp the filing date, and Rule 2.525(f)(2) (2025 amendments, In re Amendments to Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin., Fla. 2025) provides that a document placed in the correction queue may be cured within 30 days by a substantially identical refiling that "relates back to the date of the unsuccessful filing attempt." So a submission accompanied by a Request Fee Waiver receives the official file stamp upon reaching the Portal, before the clerk rules on indigency.
