PRELIMINARY NOTE ON THE GOVERNING RULE TEXT. Rule 1.500 was amended twice recently: the January 1, 2025 rewrite substituted "document" for "paper," and In re Amendments to Florida Rules of Civil Procedure 1.440 and 1.500, No. SC2022-0575 (Fla. Nov. 13, 2025), effective January 1, 2026, at 12:01 a.m., added a requirement that the party seeking default first FILE AND SERVE a motion for default (both for clerk and court defaults) and expanded the notice protections for defaulted parties. Because the default motion here was served June 29, 2026, the amended rule governs. Current text (verified against the Supreme Court amendment summary and multiple rule reprints dated April 2026):

- 1.500(a) (By the Clerk): "When a party against whom affirmative relief is sought has failed to file or serve any document in the action, the party seeking relief may file and serve a motion for default after the required time for a response has expired. The clerk must enter a default if the party against whom affirmative relief has been sought has failed to file or serve any document."
- 1.500(b) (By the Court): "When a party against whom affirmative relief is sought has failed to plead or otherwise defend as provided by these rules or any applicable statute or any order of court, the party seeking relief may file and serve a motion for default," and the court may then enter default; the rule retains the proviso that if such party "has filed or served any document in the action, that party must be served with notice of the application for default."
- 1.500(c) (Right to Plead and Right to Receive Service of Documents): "Until a default is entered against a party, all documents filed in the action or otherwise required to be served must be served on that party. A party may plead or otherwise defend at any time before default is entered. If a party against whom a default has been entered files any document after the default is entered, the clerk must notify the party of the entry of the default."
- 1.500(d): "The court may set aside a default, and if a final judgment consequent thereon has been entered, the court may set it aside in accordance with rule 1.540(b)."
- 1.500(e): Final judgments after default; notice to the defaulted party of any evidentiary hearing on unliquidated damages and jury-trial rights preserved.

QUESTION 1 — CLERK DEFAULT vs. COURT DEFAULT; WHAT COUNTS AS "ANY DOCUMENT/PAPER."

The structural rule is unchanged by the amendments: the clerk's ministerial power to default exists ONLY where the defendant "has failed to file or serve any document in the action." The disjunctive matters: filing OR service alone is enough to strip the clerk of authority. The controlling Supreme Court case is Picchi v. Barnett Bank of South Florida, N.A., 521 So. 2d 1090 (Fla. 1988) (https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/1988/70464-0.html): defense counsel filed only a notice of appearance (no responsive pleading), and the Court held that under 1.500(b) the defendant was entitled to notice of the application for default; a default obtained without that notice cannot stand (though Picchi also holds no notice of HEARING is required — see Question 4).

Florida's DCAs have given "any paper" (now "any document") an extremely liberal construction, in furtherance of the policy of adjudication on the merits, and have repeatedly held that informal papers — including bare letters to opposing counsel, never filed anywhere — preclude a clerk default and trigger the notice requirement:

- Reicheinbach v. Southeast Bank, N.A., 462 So. 2d 611 (Fla. 3d DCA 1985): a letter from a lawyer advising he had NOT YET even been retained was a served paper sufficient to require notice; "the rule does not limit the type of paper to be served."
- EGF Tampa Associates v. Bohlen, 532 So. 2d 1318 (Fla. 2d DCA 1988) (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1093233/egf-tampa-associates-v-bohlen/): a timely letter served by defendants' out-of-state (non-Florida-Bar) counsel on the plaintiffs "can constitute a paper served within the meaning of the rule and entitles the defendant to notice of the default proceedings"; default reversed. (Second DCA authority is especially weighty in Pinellas, whose pre-2023 appellate precedent comes from the Second DCA.)
- Zephyr Aviation Services, Inc. v. Amerijet International, Inc., 631 So. 2d 371 (Fla. 4th DCA 1994) (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7741243/zephyr-aviation-services-inc-v-amerijet-international-inc/): letters from California counsel to plaintiff's counsel confirming an extension and settlement talks required notice; order denying vacatur of a no-notice clerk default REVERSED.
- Gulf Maintenance & Supply, Inc. v. Barnett Bank of Tallahassee, 543 So. 2d 813 (Fla. 1st DCA 1989) (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1834737/gulf-maintenance-supply-inc-v-barnett-bank-of-tall/): default relief where defense counsel had telephoned plaintiff's counsel advising of defenses and a counterclaim; default is not appropriate where the plaintiff KNOWS the defendant is represented/intends to defend the action.

QUESTION 2 — RULE 1.500(c): PLEADING BEFORE DEFAULT IS ENTERED.

Yes. The rule text is categorical: "A party may plead or otherwise defend at any time before default is entered." The trigger is ENTRY of default, not the filing or service of the motion. Florida authorities uniformly state that if an answer or any defensive paper is filed after the motion for default but before the default is actually entered (including up to the time of the hearing on the motion), entry of default is improper; and a default entered notwithstanding a previously filed or served paper is erroneous and "must be vacated without regard to whether the defendant can establish a meritorious defense or ... inadvertence or excusable neglect." Int'l Energy Corp. v. Hackett, 687 So. 2d 941, 943 (Fla. 3d DCA 1997) (as quoted in secondary authority; see also Picchi and Zephyr, both reversing/requiring vacatur of no-notice defaults without any three-prong analysis). Practical consequence on these facts: no default has yet been entered in 26-002613-CI as of July 4, 2026. If Rodgers files her answer in the CI case on Monday, July 6 — with the $395 fee, or with an application for civil indigency/fee waiver under section 57.082, Florida Statutes (the clerk must accept the filing with a pending indigency application) — the pending motion for clerk default is defeated as a matter of law, because at the moment of any proposed entry she will have pleaded. The motion for "Clerk Default" is doubly defective because, as shown above, the clerk-route is already foreclosed by her served papers.

QUESTION 3 — STANDARD TO VACATE A DEFAULT UNDER 1.500(d)/1.540(b).

If a default is entered anyway, the movant must show (1) excusable neglect, (2) a meritorious defense, and (3) due diligence in moving promptly after learning of the default — supported by sworn affidavit. The overarching policy comes from North Shore Hospital, Inc. v. Barber, 143 So. 2d 849 (Fla. 1962) (https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/1962/31625-0.html): Florida has a "long-standing policy of liberality toward the vacating of defaults," and "if there be any reasonable doubt in the matter, it should be resolved in favor of granting the application and allowing a trial upon the merits"; appellate courts reverse a REFUSAL to vacate on a showing of (gross) abuse of discretion — a deliberately asymmetric standard favoring merits adjudication. The canonical definition of excusable neglect is Somero v. Hendry General Hospital, 467 So. 2d 1103, 1104 (Fla. 4th DCA 1985) (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1686237/somero-v-hendry-general-hosp/): "where inaction results from clerical or secretarial error, reasonable misunderstanding, a system gone awry or any other of the foibles to which human nature is heir, then upon timely application accompanied by a reasonable and credible explanation the matter should be permitted to be heard on the merits." Misdirected/misfiled papers, secretarial mistakes, and calendaring breakdowns are classic excusable neglect; by contrast, an attorney's tactical decisions, ignorance of law, or months of unexplained inaction are not (see, e.g., the Second DCA's treatment of calendaring error as excusable in NYC Construction Group, Inc. v. Jerome (Fla. 2d DCA 2022), and the converse holdings collected in practitioner sources). Applied here: a pro se defendant who (i) TIMELY prepared and e-filed a full answer, (ii) misdirected it into the companion probate case for the same decedent/property — a case in which the same law firm appeared and was actually e-served, (iii) promptly resubmitted within the clerk's 30-day correction window, and (iv) was bounced a second time solely for the $395 fee (payment refunded), presents a textbook "system gone awry"/clerical-error record, coupled with demonstrable diligence. Her meritorious defense is substantial and can be verified by affidavit: the September 13, 2025 enhanced-life-estate (Lady Bird) deed with remainder to her — an unrecorded deed is valid as between the parties and their successors with notice under Florida law, recording going to protection against creditors/BFPs (§ 695.01, Fla. Stat.), and the witness proof-of-execution (Kraft) is already accepted in the probate file. Denial of a defense based on a colorable ownership/possessory right in an ejectment action would be reversible under the North Shore liberality standard. Caveat: no appellate case squarely on wrong-CASE-NUMBER e-filing as excusable neglect was located; the argument proceeds by direct analogy to the misfiling/clerical-error cases, which are legion, and is reinforced because the opposing party actually RECEIVED the answer months before moving for default (negating any prejudice).

QUESTION 4 — NOTICE AND HEARING RIGHTS.

Under amended Rule 1.500 (eff. Jan. 1, 2026), the party seeking default must file AND SERVE a motion for default in every case — clerk or court route. Additionally, under 1.500(b), a party who "has filed or served any document in the action" must be served with notice of the application for default. Picchi, 521 So. 2d at 1091-92, holds this means notice of the APPLICATION (so the defendant can cure by pleading before entry), but does NOT require a noticed hearing on the motion; some DCAs nonetheless treat entry without any opportunity to respond as reversible where papers were served (Zephyr; EGF Tampa). A default entered by the clerk against a defendant who has served papers, or by the court without the required notice, must be set aside — without the three-prong 1.540(b) showing — because it was unauthorized when entered. Here, plaintiff served the motion June 29, which functions as the notice; the protection the rule gives Rodgers is the window it opens: she may still "plead or otherwise defend at any time before default is entered" (1.500(c)), and because her June 11 e-served answer is a "served document," the clerk cannot lawfully enter default at all — only the court can, after considering her filings. Any clerk default entered on the July 6 motion would be vacatable as a matter of law.
