## ⚠️ THE SIX QUALIFICATIONS — these changed the strategy. Read them.

### 1. The "clerk default is improper because you served papers" argument is WEAKER than first stated — use it as a secondary argument only
Rule 1.500 is **action-specific** ("failed to file or serve any document **in the action**"). Your answer was filed and e-served in the *probate* case — a different action — and the verifier found **no Florida case extending *Picchi* to wrong-case service**. Two more problems: the **January 1, 2026 amendment (SC2022-0575) deleted the old Rule 1.500(b) notice proviso** that *Picchi* construed, and *Picchi* itself holds that **serving the default motion satisfies the notice requirement** — which plaintiff did on June 29.

**What this means in practice — your two RELIABLE protections:**
- **Rule 1.500(c) (CONFIRMED):** you may plead at any time **before a default is entered**. Filing the Answer in 26-002613-CI immediately is entirely within your control and defeats the motion if no default has been entered yet. **This is the play.**
- **Rule 1.500(d)/1.540(b) (CONFIRMED):** if a default was already entered, a prompt motion to vacate on excusable neglect (the documented wrong-case e-filing + correction-queue chain — classic confirmed "system gone awry" territory) + meritorious defense (the deed/homestead) + due diligence. Keep the "your firm actually received my answer June 11" point as an equity/actual-knowledge argument, not the lead.

### 2. Relation back to June 11 has a REAL DEADLINE: ~JULY 15, 2026 — and the windows don't chain
Rule 2.525(f)(2)(A)'s text is confirmed verbatim, but relation back attaches to *the specific unsuccessful attempt being remedied*. The June 11 attempt's 30-day window runs from the **June 15 notice → ~July 15, 2026**. The second window (July 1 fee notice → ~July 31) relates back, at most, to **June 29** — after the answer deadline and the day the default motion was served. **A corrected answer successfully filed in 26-002613-CI by ~July 15 has the clean textual claim to the June 11 filing date.** Relation back must also be *asserted* — it does not self-execute against the default motion.

### 3. The clerk's $395 demand was LAWFUL for the counterclaim
Fla. The answer-alone-is-free rule (§ 28.241(3)) is **CONFIRMED**. So: **file the fee-free Answer & Affirmative Defenses alone immediately; add the counterclaim with the fee paid or an approved § 57.082 indigency application.** (The narrower argument that fee nonpayment isn't a listed correction-queue *ground* under Rule 2.525(f)(1) survives for a possible motion for review — useful, not load-bearing.)

### 4. Cite the CURRENT § 66.021 (renumbered by ch. 2018-94) in any filing
- Chain-of-title statement (complaint AND answer, copies of **every** identified instrument attached, recorded or not): **§ 66.021(7)** — *not* (4)

### 5. Jury trial: ejectment claim YES, quiet-title counterclaim NO
The jury right attaches to the legal ejectment claim only; an equitable quiet-title/declaratory counterclaim carries no jury right.

The defense becomes real the moment the answer (with the § 66.021(7) chain-of-title statement and the deed attached) is actually docketed in 26-002613-CI.

## ✅ CONFIRMED — the core of the case survives adversarial verification intact

- **Answer alone = $0** (§ 28.241(3)); no defendant appearance fee; no circuit-civil jury-demand fee
- **Rule 1.500(c)** right to plead any time before default is entered
- **Rule 1.500(d)/1.540(b)** vacate standard (excusable neglect + meritorious defense + due diligence); e-filing mishaps and clerical error are classic excusable neglect
- **Rule 2.525(f)(2)(A)** cure-by-new-document can redirect the filing into the correct case
- **§ 57.082 civil indigency**: ≤200% of federal poverty guidelines; homestead and one vehicle (≤$5,000) excluded from the asset test; ministerial, fast clerk determination; the portal supports applying at submission time
- **The PR has NO right to possession of protected homestead** (§§ 733.607(1), 733.608(1)–(2) — and § 733.608(2)'s narrow custody power doesn't apply where the home is occupied by a person claiming an interest)
- **Protected homestead is not an estate asset** and passes outside probate (Clifton v. Clifton; McKean v. Warburton line)
- **Lady Bird (enhanced life estate) deeds are valid in Florida**; the remainder vests automatically at the grantor's death, outside probate (Oglesby v. Lee; Uniform Title Standards 6.10–6.11, including homestead)
- **No notary needed for deed validity** — grantor signature + two subscribing witnesses + delivery (§ 689.01); notarization matters only for recording
- **An unrecorded deed still binds heirs and the personal representative** (§ 695.01 protects only creditors and subsequent bona-fide purchasers)
- **Recording via subscribing-witness proof of execution** (§ 695.03) is a real, available mechanism — the Kraft affidavit is that instrument
- **Caveat is moot** after PR appointment; a **Rule 5.405 petition to determine protected homestead status** is available to an "interested person"
- **Title fights with a "stranger to the estate" belong in circuit civil**, where the PR already chose to sue — answer + counterclaim there is the right forum
