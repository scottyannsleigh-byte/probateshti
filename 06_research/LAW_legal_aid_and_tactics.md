3. DEFAULT TACTICS
====================================================================

(a) OPPOSING THE MOTION FOR CLERK DEFAULT. Rule 1.500 has two tracks: 1.500(a) clerk default only "[w]hen a party against whom affirmative relief is sought has failed to file or serve ANY document"; 1.500(b) if the party "has filed or served any document in the action," default may only be entered BY THE COURT, and that party "must be served with notice of the application for default." The leading case is Picchi v. Barnett Bank of South Florida, N.A., 521 So. 2d 1090 (Fla. 1988): a paper SERVED on the plaintiff but never properly filed still defeats a clerk default and triggers the notice requirement. Here, on June 11 — within whatever response window applied — Brandi e-SERVED her Answer/Defenses/Counterclaims on Attorney Cavonis and the DeLoach firm through the Portal's e-service (and they demonstrably received it; Englander's June 30 email discusses it). The misdirection into the probate case affects FILING, not SERVICE on opposing counsel. A clerk default in 26-002613-CI would therefore be irregular, and plaintiff's counsel — with actual knowledge of her served answer — moving for a clerk default without notice is itself vulnerable.

4. RECORD THE LADY BIRD DEED NOW? YES — AND WHY, HOW, RISKS, LIS PENDENS
====================================================================

WHY NOW: Florida is a notice recording state. Fla. Stat. § 695.01(1): no conveyance "shall be good and effectual in law or equity against creditors or subsequent purchasers for a valuable consideration and without notice, unless the same be recorded." An unrecorded deed remains VALID BETWEEN THE PARTIES (and against the grantor's estate/PR, who stand in the grantor's shoes and are not BFPs), but if the PR conveys the homestead to a third-party BONA FIDE PURCHASER before her deed is of record, the BFP can cut off her remainder entirely — that is the single biggest structural risk she faces. Recording gives the world constructive notice from the moment of recording and destroys future BFP status. A deed delivered during the grantor's life may be recorded after his death; recording also creates a rebuttable presumption of delivery. Sources: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0695/Sections/0695.01.html ; https://wfgunderwriting.com/wp-content/uploads/filebase/florida/bulletins/FL01052011%20-%20Florida%20is%20a%20Notice%20State.pdf ; https://ptmlegal.com/blog/does-a-lady-bird-deed-have-to-be-recorded

HOW (the incomplete-notary problem): To be RECORDABLE, § 695.03 requires the execution to be (i) acknowledged by the grantor, OR (ii) "proved by a subscribing witness." Since the grantor is deceased and the notary block may be incomplete, the statutory cure is a sworn PROOF OF EXECUTION BY SUBSCRIBING WITNESS — Christopher Kraft (who already executed one for the probate filing) and/or Scott Rodgers appears before a notary and swears he saw the grantor sign and signed as witness; the notary certifies the proof under § 695.03. The clerk records the deed together with the witness-proof certificate. Also check § 695.26 recording formalities: names legibly printed beneath all signatures, witnesses' post-office addresses, grantee's mailing address, 3x3 blank space top right of first page. Separately, remember recordability ≠ validity: substantive validity still requires the § 689.01 formalities (grantor signature + two subscribing witnesses) and delivery — recording cures neither if truly absent, but her deed form reportedly has both witnesses. Sources: https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/695.03 ; https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/Chapter695/All

FEES/TAX: Pinellas official records: $10.00 first page, $8.50 each additional page, $1 per name over four. Documentary stamps: a lady bird/enhanced-life-estate deed conveys NO present beneficial interest, so per Florida DOR Technical Assistance Advisement 20B4-004 only the MINIMUM $0.70 doc stamp is due regardless of consideration or mortgage. So recording likely costs under $30 total. Record in person Monday at 315 Court St, Clearwater or 545 1st Ave N, St. Petersburg (both 8:30–4:30). Sources: https://www.mypinellasclerk.gov/Home/Fee-Schedule ; https://floridarevenue.com/TaxLaw/Documents/20B4-004.pdf ; https://www.deeds.com/recorder/florida/pinellas/

RISKS OF RECORDING: If the deed is genuine, recording it is lawful and standard; there is no sanction for recording a deed late or after death. The estate will predictably attack it (forgery/undue influence/lack of delivery/defective execution), and Fla. Stat. § 817.535 criminalizes recording instruments KNOWN to be false or fraudulent — a risk only if the deed were fabricated, not for a good-faith claimant. Recording may sharpen the litigation (expect the PR to add a quiet-title count), but the alternative — staying off record while the PR markets the property — is far worse. Doc-stamp exposure is de minimis per the TAA. One more angle worth flagging to whatever lawyer she gets: the property was the decedent's HOMESTEAD; Florida homestead generally passes outside probate administration and a PR's power to sell it is limited, which is an independent brake on a quick PR sale.

LIS PENDENS: Yes — her counterclaim (quiet title / declaratory judgment establishing the deed and her title) is exactly the kind of claim that supports a NOTICE OF LIS PENDENS under Fla. Stat. § 48.23. Effect: anyone who buys or lends against the property after recording of the lis pendens takes subject to the outcome of her case — it neutralizes the BFP threat even beyond deed recording and as a practical matter freezes marketability. Mechanics: record the notice (case caption, case number, legal description — Lot 271, Oakhurst Groves, Plat Book 46, pp. 16–17, Parcel 19-30-15-62658-000-2710) in the official records AND file it in the court file; it must be signed. Caveat: where the action is NOT "founded on a duly recorded instrument," § 48.23(3) lets the court control the lis pendens — discharge it or condition it on a BOND — and the proponent must show a "fair nexus" between the claimed property interest and the suit (Chiusolo v. Kennedy, 614 So. 2d 491 (Fla. 1993)). Her nexus is obvious (she claims record title via the deed), but SEQUENCE MATTERS: if she records the lady bird deed FIRST and pleads the counterclaim as founded on that now-recorded instrument, she has a strong argument that the lis pendens is founded on a duly recorded instrument and is not dischargeable or bondable at all. Recommended order of operations Monday: (1) record the deed + witness proof at the clerk's official records counter; (2) file/record the notice of lis pendens referencing the recorded deed's OR book/page; (3) confirm answer/opposition are docketed in 26-002613-CI. Sources: https://www.jimersonfirm.com/blog/2021/01/lis-pendens-florida/ ; https://gulisanolaw.com/the-improper-use-of-lis-pendens-in-florida/ ; https://www.davidsteinfeld.com/how-to-remove-lis-pendens-florida.html

**Verifier's analysis:** The abstract statement of the clerk-default/court-default dichotomy is largely correct, but the claim is materially misleading in three ways as applied to these facts, and its description of Picchi is factually wrong.

1. VERIFIED RULE TEXT — the rule is action-specific. Current Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.500 (as amended by In re Amendments to Fla. Rules of Civ. Pro. 1.440 and 1.500, No. SC2025-XXXX (Fla. 2025), effective Jan. 1, 2026, verified at https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-supreme-court/117932137.html and https://floridajustice.com/rule/1-500-defaults-and-final-judgments/): (a) applies when a party "has failed to file or serve any document IN THE ACTION," and (b)'s notice proviso applies only "if such party has filed or served any document IN THE ACTION." Brandi filed and e-served her Answer in the PROBATE case, 26-000874-ES — a different action from the ejectment case, 26-002613-CI, where the default was sought. The CI docket contains no document filed or served by her. Neither Picchi nor any authority located addresses wrong-case service; a targeted search found no Florida case holding that service of a paper in a different case constitutes appearance "in the action" for Rule 1.500 purposes. Plaintiff can plausibly argue the plain text is not triggered. Calling this "strong grounds" under Picchi/1.500(a) overstates it; it is at best a contestable functional-appearance argument (the answer, responsive on its face to the ejectment complaint, was actually e-served on the same firm representing the plaintiff), not settled law.

2. VERIFIED PICCHI — the parenthetical misdescribes the case. I read the actual slip opinion (http://library.law.fsu.edu/Digital-Collections/flsupct/dockets/70464/op-70464.pdf; see also https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/1988/70464-0.html). Picchi did NOT involve a "served-but-unfiled motion." The defendant FILED a notice of appearance; plaintiff then FILED AND SERVED a motion for default and obtained default without a hearing. The Supreme Court held 1.500(b) requires notice of the application for default but does NOT require notice of hearing — i.e., serving the default motion itself satisfies the notice requirement — and it vacated the default only for retroactivity-fairness reasons within the Fourth District, while condemning nonresponsive papers filed to avoid default as abuse of process. So Picchi's holding actually cuts partly against the claim here: plaintiff's counsel SERVED the "Motion for Clerk Default w Default" on Brandi on June 29, which under Picchi is the very notice 1.500(b) requires; and under the 2026 amendment even a clerk default now requires a filed-and-served motion, which plaintiff satisfied. Her remaining Picchi-based argument is narrow: that only the court, not the clerk, may enter the default — and that argument depends entirely on winning point 1 (that the probate-case e-service counts as a document "in the action").

3. THE GENUINELY STRONG GROUNDS ARE DIFFERENT. Rule 1.500(c) lets her plead at ANY time before default is actually entered — filing the answer in 26-002613-CI (with the $395 fee) on Monday July 6, if default has not yet been entered, defeats the motion outright. And if default has been entered, Rule 1.500(d)/1.540(b) vacatur for excusable neglect (pro se wrong-case e-filing, documented diligent correction attempts through the clerk's correction queue, prompt action, meritorious defense via the claimed Lady Bird deed) under the liberal North Shore Hospital v. Barber standard is the reliable route — not Picchi. The secondary-source citations offered (douglasfirm.com, stopforeclosurelawyer.com) accurately restate the general dichotomy but do not address wrong-case service either.
